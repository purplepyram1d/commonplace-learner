# 00 — Start Here: Gear & Setup

Read this before Day 1. It covers what you need, what you don't, how to set up your space, and what to expect from the first month.

---

## 1. What you need (the essentials)

| Item | Why | Rough cost (USD) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acoustic guitar** | Obviously | $150–$400 for a good beginner instrument | See "Choosing a guitar" below |
| **Clip-on tuner** | Your guitar goes out of tune constantly, and a guitar that's out of tune makes everything sound wrong, including your singing | $10–$25 | Snark, D'Addario, or TC Electronic Polytune Clip. Free phone apps work too (GuitarTuna, Fender Tune, Pano Tuner) |
| **Picks**, a variety pack | For strumming | $5 | Start with **thin (0.46–0.60 mm)** for strumming. Buy medium (0.71–0.88 mm) too so you can compare |
| **Metronome** | Keeps your time honest | Free | Phone app (Pro Metronome, Soundbrenner, or Google "metronome") |
| **Phone for recording** | You need to hear yourself from the outside | Free | Voice Memos or any recorder app. Video is even better because you can see your hands and posture |
| **Water** | For your voice | Free | Room temperature, sipped throughout |
| **A chair without arms** | Correct playing posture | — | A kitchen chair is perfect. Not a sofa, and not the bed |

## 2. Nice to have (not required in Month 1)

| Item | Why | When to buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Capo** | Changes the key so the song fits your voice | Buy one now if you have a higher voice (see the song guide). Otherwise it's optional. $10–$20 (Kyser, Shubb, D'Addario NS) |
| **Guitar strap** | For playing standing up | Week 4 if you want to perform standing |
| **Guitar stand** | **Highly recommended.** A guitar left out on a stand gets played; a guitar in its case doesn't | Anytime, $15–$25 |
| **Spare set of strings** | Strings break, and new strings sound much better | When the first set is 2–3 months old |
| **Humidifier (in-case or soundhole)** | Protects solid-wood guitars in dry climates or heated winter rooms | If you live somewhere dry |
| **Music stand or tablet holder** | Holds the chord chart at eye level, which stops you hunching over | Week 3 |
| **Headphones** | For playing along with the recording without disturbing others | Anytime |

## 3. What NOT to buy yet

- An expensive guitar. Wait until you know you'll stick with it.
- Pedals, amps, or an electric guitar.
- Courses and apps with subscriptions. Everything you need for Month 1 is in this folder.
- Thick "heavy" picks (1.0 mm and up). They make strumming harder for beginners.
- Heavy-gauge strings. They're harder on your fingers.

## 4. Choosing a guitar (if you don't already have one)

### Size
- **Dreadnought**: the standard big acoustic. Loud and full-sounding. Fine for most adults.
- **Concert / Grand Auditorium / "OM"**: slightly smaller body, more comfortable for smaller people and for sitting. **A great choice for a singer-guitarist.**
- **Parlor / 3/4 size / travel**: small and comfortable, with a quieter, thinner sound. Good if you're shorter than about 5'2" / 157 cm or for kids.

### Steel strings vs. nylon (classical)
- This course assumes a **steel-string acoustic**, the guitar Bill Withers' song is associated with.
- Nylon strings are softer on the fingers but have a **wider neck**, which makes chords like G harder for small hands. They're a fine choice if your fingers are very sensitive, and everything in this course still works on one.

### What to look for when buying
1. **Low action**: the strings should be close to the fretboard. High action is the #1 reason beginners quit, because it makes pressing strings painful. A good music shop will do a **"setup"** (adjust the action) for $40–$70. **This is the best money you'll spend.** Ask for it.
2. **Solid top** (vs. laminate): sounds better and improves with age. Nice to have, not essential.
3. **The tuners hold pitch**: tune it in the shop, play for 5 minutes, and check whether it's still in tune.
4. **No buzzing** when you play each string open and at a few frets.
5. **Straight neck**: sight down it from the headstock end. It should look straight or very slightly curved (a slight curve is normal).

### Reliable beginner brands (as of this writing)
Yamaha (FG800, FS800, FG830), Fender (CD-60S, CD-140S), Epiphone (DR-100, Songmaker), Cort (AD810, Earth series), Taylor (GS Mini, Academy series) and Seagull (S6) at the upper end.

### If you already have a guitar
- **Put new strings on it** if you don't know how old the strings are. Dead strings sound dull and won't stay in tune. Light gauge (.012–.053) or **extra-light (.010–.047)** for Month 1. Extra-lights are easier on your fingers.
- If it's hard to press the strings down, take it to a shop for a setup.

## 5. Set up your practice space

The single biggest predictor of whether you practice is **how easy it is to start.**

- [ ] Guitar **out of its case**, on a stand or wall hanger, in a room you walk through every day
- [ ] Armless chair right next to it
- [ ] Tuner clipped to the headstock or kept next to the guitar
- [ ] Picks in one place (a small dish, or tucked between the strings at the headstock)
- [ ] Phone stand or holder for recording (a stack of books works)
- [ ] A printed copy of the day's plan, or a tablet showing it
- [ ] Water bottle
- [ ] Good light, so you can see the fretboard
- [ ] Ideally, a room where you **don't feel self-conscious singing**. Being afraid of being heard makes people sing timidly, and timid singing is breathy and out of tune. If you share a space, practice when others are out, or tell them "I'm learning; you're going to hear some bad singing for a month," which takes the pressure off.

## 6. What to expect: an honest timeline

| When | What it feels like |
|---|---|
| Days 1–4 | Fingertips sting. Chords sound buzzy. Everything feels awkward, and your voice feels exposed. **Totally normal.** |
| Days 5–10 | Fingertips hurt less and start to harden. Em and Am start sounding clean. Chord changes are slow (a noticeable gap). |
| Days 10–14 | Callouses form. G and Dm are frustrating. Your singing feels steadier. Strumming and counting together feels weird. |
| Days 15–21 | Changes get faster. You can play the song's progression. Singing *and* playing at the same time feels like patting your head and rubbing your belly. **This is the "wall," and everyone hits it. The method in [04 — Singing While Playing](04-Singing-While-Playing.md) gets you through it.** |
| Days 22–28 | Suddenly it clicks. Maybe not perfect, but you can get through the whole song. You'll hear yourself on a recording and think "that's actually a song." |

### Things that are normal
- Sore fingertips (for about 2–3 weeks)
- Indentations or grooves in your fingertips after practice
- Hating the sound of your own recorded voice. Everyone does. Your voice sounds different inside your head because of bone conduction, and the recording is what everyone else hears. You'll get used to it.
- Days where you feel worse than yesterday. Progress isn't a straight line, and plateaus come right before jumps.
- Your guitar going out of tune every session, especially with new strings. Tune every single time you pick it up.

### Things that are NOT normal (stop and see [Troubleshooting](07-Troubleshooting-and-FAQ.md))
- Sharp pain in your wrist, thumb base, or forearm
- Numbness or tingling in your hand
- Blisters that open or bleed
- A voice that's hoarse the next morning, pain when swallowing, or losing your voice
- Hoarseness lasting more than 2 weeks (see a doctor, ideally an ENT/laryngologist)

## 7. Before your very first session: 10-minute checklist

- [ ] Guitar strung, in its spot, tuner ready
- [ ] Downloaded a metronome app
- [ ] Downloaded a tuner app (a backup, and it also works as a pitch checker for your voice)
- [ ] Found the original recording of "Ain't No Sunshine" on your music app and saved it
- [ ] Found the lyrics from a licensed source and saved or printed them
- [ ] Created your practice log (see [08 — Practice Log Template](08-Practice-Log-Template.md))
- [ ] Recorded a 30-second **"Day 0" video**: say hello, strum the open strings, and sing "Happy Birthday" or any song you know. **Don't skip this.** On Day 28 you'll watch it and be amazed.
- [ ] Trimmed your fretting-hand fingernails short (see [01 — Guitar Fundamentals](01-Guitar-Fundamentals.md#fingernails))
