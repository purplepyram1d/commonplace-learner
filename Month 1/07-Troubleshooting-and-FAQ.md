# 07 — Troubleshooting & FAQ

Find your problem, try the fixes in order, and if nothing works, skip ahead and come back to it tomorrow. Many problems solve themselves after a night's sleep.

---

## Guitar: sound problems

### "My chord buzzes"
1. **Press closer to the fret** (just behind it, on the side toward the headstock).
2. **Use your fingertips**, not the pads. Trim your nails if they stop you.
3. **Press a little harder**, or check whether one finger is barely touching.
4. Check for a finger **lightly touching a neighboring string**.
5. If an **open string** buzzes even with no fingers on it, the guitar may need a **setup** (low action or uneven frets). Take it to a shop.
6. Old, dead strings buzz and sound thin. Restring.

### "One string in the chord is silent or dead (muted)"
- Pluck each string individually to find which one.
- Usually the underside of a neighboring finger is resting on it. **Arch your fingers more.** Bring your wrist forward (under the neck) and your thumb lower behind the neck.
- **Am:** finger 1 mutes string 1. **Dm:** finger 3 mutes string 1. **G:** finger 1 mutes string 4, or finger 2 mutes string 5.

### "The chord sounds wrong/sour even though my fingers are right"
- **Tune the guitar.** (This is the answer 80% of the time.)
- Check the fret code again: right frets, right strings?
- Check you aren't strumming strings marked **x** (e.g. the low E on Am, or the low E and A on Dm).

### "Strumming sounds harsh or clunky"
- Grip the pick a little more loosely, and show less tip.
- Angle the pick slightly so it glides.
- Try a **thinner pick** (0.46–0.60 mm).
- Strum from the forearm and wrist, not the elbow.
- Strum over the end of the fretboard (near the neck) for a warmer sound.

### "My guitar won't stay in tune"
- **New strings** stretch for days. Stretch them (gently pull each one away from the fretboard), retune, and repeat.
- **Always tune up to the note** (go below, then come up).
- Temperature and humidity changes (heaters, AC, windows) move the tuning.
- Check that the strings are wound properly on the posts (a few wraps, going downward).
- If a peg feels loose or slips, a shop can tighten or replace it.

### "The string broke"
It happens. Don't panic. Change it (see [01 — Guitar Fundamentals §15](01-Guitar-Fundamentals.md#15-changing-strings)). If you don't have a spare, change the whole set. You can keep practicing with 5 strings until then (most chords still mostly work).

## Guitar: technique problems

### "My chord changes are too slow"
- **One-Minute Changes daily.** It works. Trust the numbers in your log.
- Move **all fingers together**, not one at a time.
- Use **anchor and guide fingers** ([01 §10](01-Guitar-Fundamentals.md#10-changing-chords)).
- **Look ahead:** start thinking about the next chord while playing the current one.
- Use the **open-string cheat**: lift early on the last up-strum.
- Practice the change in the air (air changes).
- Use easy G temporarily.
- **Slow the tempo** so the changes fit, and speed up by 5 BPM once it's clean 3× in a row.

### "I can't do G / my fingers won't stretch"
- Use **easy G** (`xx0003`) for now, which is totally valid.
- Shape-building for full G: 3 minutes daily. Form it, hold for 10 s, release, repeat.
- Thumb lower behind the neck (middle of the neck's back), wrist further forward.
- Fingers need to come **down from above**, like hammers, not reach sideways.
- Stretch gently: fingers apart, palm against a table, 15 s.
- It really does come. Most people get full G at around 2–4 weeks.

### "I keep speeding up / slowing down"
- **Use the metronome** every day for strum practice.
- **Count out loud.** It's the best fix there is.
- Speeding up is usually excitement or rushing to "get to" the easy part. Slowing down is usually in front of a hard chord change. Isolate that change.
- Record yourself with the metronome and listen: where do you drift?

### "I can't keep the strumming pattern going"
- Say the pattern out loud ("DOWN, down-up, up-down-up").
- Keep the hand **moving constantly** (a pendulum), hitting or missing the strings.
- Practice it on one chord (no changes) until it's automatic, then add changes.
- Drop to quarter notes for the song and practice the pattern separately. It'll merge.

### "My up-strums miss / hit too many strings"
- Up-strums should only catch the **top 3–4 strings**, and that's correct.
- Loosen the pick grip; let the pick drag lightly.

## Guitar: physical problems

### "My fingertips hurt"
- **Normal for the first 2–3 weeks.** They're building callouses.
- Split practice into short sessions (2 × 15 min).
- Use extra-light strings.
- Stop before blisters form. **Don't play through open blisters.** Rest the fingers 1–2 days (do voice work, listening and mental practice instead).
- A lower action (shop setup) reduces the pressure you need.

### "My wrist/forearm/thumb hurts"
- **This is not normal. Stop playing today.**
- Common causes: gripping the neck too hard, a sharply bent wrist, a thumb squeezing too hard, a tense shoulder, or bad posture (e.g. guitar tilted back).
- Next session: less pressure (do the "minimum pressure" exercise), check your posture, add warm-ups, and take breaks every 10 min.
- If the pain lasts more than a few days, or you have **numbness or tingling**, see a doctor or physiotherapist.

### "My back/neck/shoulders ache"
- Posture check: front half of the chair, tall spine, feet flat, guitar not tilted back, shoulders down.
- Don't hunch to look at the fretboard.
- Take breaks and do the Body Warm-up routine.

## Voice problems

### "I can't tell if I'm in tune"
- Use a **tuner or pitch app** on your voice: it tells you exactly.
- **Cover one ear**, or cup your hand behind it, to hear yourself better.
- **Record yourself** and listen back. It's much easier to hear pitch when you're not singing.
- Hum first (easier to match), then open to a vowel.
- **Slide** into the note from below, slowly, until it "locks."
- Practice matching single notes daily. Pitch accuracy is trainable and improves steadily.

### "I'm always flat (under the note)"
- More breath energy: sing with more support, not more push.
- Think "brighter" or "higher" on each note, or smile slightly.
- Don't sing too quietly. Timid singing drifts flat.
- Check that your posture isn't collapsed.
- Check that the guitar is in tune!

### "I'm always sharp (over the note)"
- Less so for beginners, but it happens with tension and pushing. Relax the jaw, neck and shoulders. Sing a bit softer.

### "My voice cracks or breaks"
- On a high note: you're hitting your chest/head transition. Lighten the sound as you go up; don't push.
- Consider using the capo to shift the key, or moving to the octave that fits.
- Daily sirens and lip trills smooth the break over time.
- If it's cracking on **normal** notes, your voice is tired. Rest.

### "My throat hurts / feels scratchy / I'm hoarse"
- **Stop singing for today.** Hydrate. Breathe steam. Speak less.
- Next time: shorter singing blocks, always warm up, sing softer, and check for tension (jaw, neck, tongue).
- Next-morning hoarseness means you overdid it the day before.
- **See a doctor (ENT) if hoarseness lasts more than 2 weeks, or if singing is painful.**

### "I run out of breath"
- Breathe **at every turnaround**. Don't skip breath points.
- Breathe **low** (belly), not high (chest and shoulders).
- Don't start phrases too loud.
- Hiss ladder daily; your time will grow.
- Breathy tone wastes air. Aim for a clearer, more "focused" tone (humming first helps find it).

### "I hate the sound of my voice"
- Almost everyone does at first. You're hearing yourself without bone conduction for the first time.
- Keep recording daily. Within 2 weeks it becomes "just your voice."
- Compare early recordings with later ones: the progress is real.

### "I'm embarrassed to sing where people can hear"
- Practice at times when you're alone, or in a car (great for singing along).
- Tell your housemates you're learning. Removing the "secret" removes a lot of the embarrassment.
- Everybody who sings well started out singing badly where someone could hear them.

## Coordination problems (singing + playing)

### "When I start singing, my strumming stops"
The #1 problem, and the most normal. Go back through the [Layering Method](04-Singing-While-Playing.md#2-the-layering-method-the-core-technique):
1. Play + count out loud → 2. Play + talk → 3. Play + hum → 4. Play + speak lyrics → 5. Sing over **whole notes** → 6. Quarter notes → 7. Full pattern

### "My hand follows the rhythm of my words"
- Speak the lyrics over quarter-note downs, very slowly. Notice which syllables fall **between** strums.
- Drill B (off-beat speech).
- Keep your hand moving as a pendulum. The pendulum is sacred.

### "I come in too late / too early"
- Map the **pickup notes**. Some lines start before the chord.
- Count "1, 2, 3, 4" out loud in the previous bar and start singing at the right count.
- Loop the transition: the end of one line into the start of the next.

### "I lose my place in the song"
- Use the one-page performance chart.
- Know the structure: Intro, V1, V2, Bridge, V3, Outro. Say the section name in your head as you start each.
- Know the landmarks: **Dm happens once per verse, on Line 4.** When you hit Dm, you know you're near the end of a verse.

### "I can do each part alone, but together everything falls apart"
- **Slow down.** Drop the tempo 10–15 BPM.
- Simplify the strum.
- Work in small chunks (1 line, then 2 lines, then a verse).
- Sleep on it. Coordination improves overnight.

## General FAQ

**How long should I practice each day?**
20–45 minutes, depending on the week, capped at 30 on post-shift mornings (see [README](README.md#your-schedule)). Consistency matters much more than duration.

**I missed a day (or three). What do I do?**
Don't try to "catch up" by doubling the next session. Continue from where you stopped. If you missed 3+ days, repeat the last day you completed. Adding a few days to the month is completely fine: the goal is the song, not the calendar.

**I'm behind schedule. Should I move on anyway?**
If you're **mostly** there (e.g. G changes at 20/min instead of 30), move on. You'll keep practicing those changes in the song. If a skill is really not there (e.g. you can't form Dm at all), take 2–3 more days on it. Using simpler versions (easy G, whole-note strums) is always allowed.

**I'm ahead of schedule. Can I go faster?**
Yes, but don't skip the independence and layering steps. Use your extra time for: the bonus bass walk, the swing feel, learning the song's details by ear, or starting a second song.

**Should I learn to read standard music notation?**
Not for Month 1. Chord charts and fret codes cover what you need for strumming songs. It's useful later if you're interested.

**Do I need lessons with a teacher?**
Not required, but **even one or two lessons** are very valuable for checking posture, hand position, and vocal technique. A teacher can spot problems you can't see. Consider one around Week 2 or 3.

**Should I use apps or YouTube as well?**
Tuner, metronome and pitch apps: yes. For lessons, focus on this plan for Month 1. Jumping between many courses is a classic way to stall. Watching a video to *see* a technique (e.g. restringing, the strumming motion) is great.

**Pick or fingers?**
A pick for Month 1 (it's easier to learn consistent strumming). Try thumb-strumming in Week 4 for this song's mellow sound.

**What if the song is too low or too high for me?**
See [Song Guide §8: Finding your key](05-Song-Guide-Aint-No-Sunshine.md#finding-your-key). Capo up, sing an octave up, or detune.

**I'm a woman. Should I change the key?**
Most female voices find the original key low. Try singing an octave up first. If that's too high, try capo 3–5 until it feels right. Your ears are the judge.

**Can I play along with the recording?**
Only if you're in the original key (no capo) and in tune with the record (it's tuned close to standard pitch, but vintage recordings can be slightly off, so check). Playing along is a great test of tempo in Week 4.

**Is it OK to look at my hands?**
Yes in Weeks 1–3. Glance with your eyes instead of dropping your head. In Week 4, practice looking up for at least some of the song.

**What about sitting vs. standing?**
Sit for the whole month. Standing is optional in Week 4 (with a strap at sitting height).

**My fingers are too big/small/short for guitar.**
Almost certainly not. Players with all hand sizes play at every level. Small hands: a smaller-bodied guitar or a slightly narrower neck helps. Large fingers: very arched fingers and precise tip placement. Adjustments help, but it's a practice issue, not a hand issue.

**I'm older / I've never been "musical." Can I really do this?**
Yes. Bill Withers himself didn't start his music career until he was in his thirties. Adults learn instruments all the time. Adults are often better at structured practice than kids. The only real requirement is doing the practice.
