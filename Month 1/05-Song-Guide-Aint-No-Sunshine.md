# 05 — Song Guide: "Ain't No Sunshine"

**Artist:** Bill Withers · **Album:** *Just As I Am* (1971) · **Producer:** Booker T. Jones
**Key:** A minor · **Time:** 4/4 · **Tempo:** ~76–80 BPM, relaxed and lightly swung · **Length:** ~2 minutes
**Chords:** Am, Em (or Em7), G, Dm

> **About the lyrics:** they're copyrighted and not reproduced here. Get them from a licensed source (Spotify/Apple Music lyrics view, Musixmatch, the album sleeve). This guide gives you the **map**: chords, bars, strums, breaths, and sections. You lay the words over it. Print the lyrics with wide line spacing so you can pencil chords and breath marks above them.

> **Trust your ears over any chart, including this one.** Chord charts online disagree on small details, such as where exactly the Em/G turnaround lands or whether it's Em or Em7. This guide gives a clean, beginner-friendly arrangement that sounds right with the song. In Week 4, listen closely to the record and nudge your timing to match what you hear. That skill is called "learning by ear," and it starts here.

---

## 1. A little background (it helps you sing it)

Bill Withers was a 32-year-old factory worker (he installed toilets in airplanes) when he recorded this, his debut single. It became a huge hit and won a Grammy for Best R&B Song. The famous stretch where he repeats "I know" was meant to be a placeholder until he wrote more lyrics. The other musicians told him to leave it as it was. He was an ordinary person who picked up a guitar in his late twenties, which is a pretty good story for a beginner.

The song is about absence and longing, but it's **understated**. The emotion comes from restraint, not volume. That's great news: **you don't need a big voice to sing this well.**

| Chord | Role | Feeling |

| **Em / Em7** | Moving away (v) | Restless |
| **G** | Pushing back home (VII) | Lifts, leads back to Am |
| **Dm** | The emotional peak (iv) | The ache. It shows up on the 4th line of each verse |

## 3. Song structure (map)

| # | Section | Lines | Chords (summary) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Intro** | — | Am | 2 bars (our arrangement) |
| 2 | **Verse 1** | 5 lines | Am-Em-G-Am ×3, then Em-Dm, then Am-Em-G-Am | The title line opens it |
| 3 | **Verse 2** | 5 lines | Same as Verse 1 | Opens with two "wondering" lines instead of the title line |
| 4 | **Bridge: "I know"** | "I know" ×26, then 2 lines | Am (held throughout the "I know"s), then the verse-line pattern | The famous section |
| 5 | **Verse 3** | 5 lines | Same as Verse 1 | Returns to the title line |
| 6 | **Outro** | The last line repeated | Am-Em-G-Am, end on Am | Slow down; let the last Am ring |

That's really only **one musical pattern** (the verse) plus the one-chord "I know" section. If you can play one verse, you can play almost the entire song.

## 4. The chord chart (beginner arrangement)

**How to read it:** `|` = bar line. Each bar = 4 beats. `Am . . .` = Am for 4 beats. `Em . G .` = Em for 2 beats, then G for 2 beats. Write your lyrics under each bar.

### Intro (2 bars)
```
| Am . . . | Am . . . |
  1 2 3 4    1 2 3 4
```
Count yourself in first: say "1, 2, 3, 4" out loud (or in your head) before strumming.

### Verse pattern (used for Verses 1, 2 and 3)
```
Line 1:  | Am . . . | Em . G . |      ← title line (or 1st "wondering" line in V2)
Line 2:  | Am . . . | Em . G . |      
Line 3:  | Am . . . | Em . G . |      ← title line again
Line 4:  | Em . . . | Dm . . . |      ← the "always gone too long" line (V1) / its V2 & V3 equivalent
Line 5:  | Am . . . | Em . G . |      ← the "anytime she goes away" line
```
Then the next section begins on **Am**.

**What's happening in each line:**
- The singer's words mostly fall over the **Am** bar, and the line ends as the chord moves.
- The **Em → G** in the second bar is a **turnaround**: a quick move that "leans back" into the next Am. It fills the gap between vocal phrases. **That's where you breathe.**
- On the recording the Em–G turnaround is quick and tucks in right before the next line. In our beginner arrangement it gets 2 beats each so you have time to change. Once you're comfortable (Week 4), listen and tighten it up to match.

### Bridge: the "I know" section
```
| Am . . . | Am . . . | Am . . . | Am . . . |  ... (keep going on Am until the "I know"s end)
```
- The chord stays on **Am** for all 26 "I know"s.
- **Your job:** listen to the record with a count going: how many bars of Am does the section last? Write it here: **_____ bars**. Counting it yourself makes you really know the song.
- Then two lines follow (a "leave that young thing alone" line, then a return of the title line). Use the **same pattern as a normal verse line** for each: `| Am . . . | Em . G . |`.

### Verse 3
Same as the Verse pattern. (Words: title line; a "darkness" line; title line; the same Line 4 as V2; the "anytime" line.)

### Outro
```
| Am . . . | Em . G . |   ← repeat the "anytime" line as the record does
| Am . . . | Em . G . |
| Am  (let ring) ………… |
```
**Slow down** over the last 2 bars (this is called a *ritardando*, or "rit.") and finish with **one strum of Am**. Let it ring until the sound fades completely. Don't kill it early. The silence at the end is part of the performance.

## 6. Strumming arrangement

Different sections get different energy. Start with **Level 1** everywhere and add levels as the weeks go.

### Level 1: Whole notes (Week 3, and for learning to sing it)
One strum on beat 1 of each chord, let it ring.
```
| Am . . . | Em . G . |
  ↓          ↓   ↓
```

### Level 2: Quarter-note downs (Week 3)
```
| Am . . . | Em . G . |
  ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓    ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓
```

### Level 3: The folk pattern (Week 3–4, **the main target**)
On a 4-beat Am bar:
```
  1   &   2   &   3   &   4   &
  D       D   U       U   D   U
```
On a split bar (Em 2 beats + G 2 beats), use a half-pattern for each chord:
```
  1   &   2   &  |  3   &   4   &
  D       D   U  |  D       D   U
  Em             |  G
```
The **up-strum on the last "&"** before a chord change is where you "cheat": lift your fingers early and let the up-strum catch open strings. (See [01 — Guitar Fundamentals](01-Guitar-Fundamentals.md#10-changing-chords).)

### Level 4: Record feel (Week 4, optional)
- **Swing** the 8ths: lazy, laid-back "&"s.
- Play with your **thumb** or softly with a pick near the neck for a warmer tone.
- **Accent beat 2 and 4** very slightly (the soul "backbeat").

### Section-by-section dynamics (Week 4)
| Section | Strum | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | Level 1 or 2 | Soft |
| Verse 1 | Level 2 or 3 | Soft/medium, intimate |
| Verse 2 | Level 3 | Medium |
| Bridge ("I know") | **Quarter-note downs, palm-muted or light**, building louder bar by bar | Soft → building |
| Verse 3 | Level 3 | Fullest, most intense |
| Outro | Back to Level 1 | Soft, slowing down, final ringing Am |

## 7. Optional: the turnaround bass walk (Week 4 bonus)

Instead of strumming the Em–G turnaround, try **picking single bass notes** that walk back to Am. It sounds great and it imitates the bass line's movement under the song.

Pick the low E string open on beat 1 (while holding Em), the low E string at fret 3 on beat 3 (while holding G), then strum Am on beat 1. Bass notes **E → G → A** rise up to "home." Once that's easy, add a strum after each bass note: **bass-strum, bass-strum.**

## 8. Singing it: vocal notes

### Finding your key
1. **Sing along with the original recording** (no guitar). Which of these happens?
   - **Comfortable, not straining, not too low:** 👍 Play with **no capo** in Am.
   - **Too low** (you're rumbling, losing volume or clarity on low notes; common for higher voices): try **singing an octave up** first. If that's too high, **put a capo on fret 3, 4 or 5** and play the same shapes. Test by singing Verse 1 with each and pick the one where the lowest notes are clear and the highest notes are easy.
   - **Too high** (straining on the highest notes; rarer for this song): you'd need to go **lower** than Am, which a capo can't do. Options: (a) sing the highest phrases a bit softer and lighter, (b) in Month 2, learn the song in a lower key such as G minor or F♯ minor (these need barre chords), or (c) tune the whole guitar down a half step or a whole step (a common pro trick; use your tuner to tune to E♭–A♭–D♭–G♭–B♭–E♭ for a half step down).
2. Write your chosen capo position on your performance chart.

**Test notes:** the capo moves everything up. If you choose capo 3, your "Am" sounds as C minor. So when you check your starting pitch with a tuner app, the letters will be different from the chord shape names. That's expected.

### Finding your starting note
Your first sung note has to come from **somewhere**. Here's how to find it every time:
1. Play the record, and pause the instant after the first word.
2. Hum the note you just heard.
3. Find that note on your guitar's **open strings or the Am chord**. Pluck each string of Am one at a time and hum along. Which one matches your note (or is its octave)?
4. **Write it down** ("my starting note = string __ in the Am chord").
5. Before you sing, **pluck that string and hum it** during your intro bars. Singers call this "getting your note," and every professional does it.

### Phrase by phrase
| Part | Vocal focus |
|---|---|
| **Title line** | Relaxed, conversational. Let the long vowel sounds ("sun," "gone") sit on the vowel. Don't close to the consonants early |
| **Lines 2 & 3** | Same energy; keep your pitch steady rather than drifting flat |
| **Line 4 (over Em → Dm)** | The emotional moment. A little more intensity and a slightly longer note, with a feeling of reaching |
| **Line 5** | Resolves, like a sigh back to home |
| **"Wondering" lines (V2)** | Softer, more questioning |
| **"I know" section** | Rhythmic, percussive, almost spoken-sung. Short "I know" repetitions. **This is a breathing test:** see below |
| **Verse 3** | Fullest version. More commitment, still not shouting |
| **Outro** | Softer, slowing, fading |

### Breath plan
Mark these on your lyric sheet with a **✓**:
- **Before every line**, during the Em–G turnaround. (That's why the turnaround exists: it's your breathing space.)
- **The "I know" section:** there are 26 "I know"s in a row. **Don't try to do them in one breath.** Plan quick "surprise breaths" (see [Voice Fundamentals §3](02-Voice-Fundamentals.md#3-breathing)) every 4–6 repetitions. Listen to the record: where does Withers breathe? Copy that, and write the breaths in. Some singers breathe in groups: "I know ×6 ✓ ×6 ✓ ×6 ✓ ×8." Find what works.
- **Keep counting the "I know"s.** Losing count is the most common mistake here, and it's why you mapped the section's bar length. Your fingers are busy, so count in your head, in groups.

### The "I know" section: a mini-lesson
1. **Speak** "I know" in rhythm with the record, not singing, just speaking. Count the reps.
2. **Speak it while strumming Am** (quarter-note downs). Keep the strum steady whatever your mouth does.
3. **Sing it lightly** on the pitch you hear, while strumming.
4. **Add breaths** at your planned points.
5. **Add the build:** start quiet, and grow louder and more intense toward the end, with the strumming growing too.

## 9. The most common problems with this song (and fixes)

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| **G → Am change too slow** | Isolate it: One-Minute Changes on G ↔ Am daily. Use easy G in Weeks 2–3 |
| **Em → Dm change too slow** | Isolate it. Practice forming Dm in the air. Hold Em for its full bar, and use the "open-string cheat" on the last up-strum |
| **Rushing the turnaround** | Count "1 2 **3** 4" out loud. G lands on **3**. Practice with a metronome |
| **Starting to sing late** (waiting for the chord) | Many lines start with a pickup, just before the chord. Count out loud and practice the pickup (see [04 — Singing While Playing](04-Singing-While-Playing.md#pickup-notes-singing-before-the-chord)) |
| **Losing count in the "I know" section** | Map the bars; count in groups; watch the number of bars rather than counting "I know"s |
| **Running out of breath** | Plan breaths; take lower, quicker breaths in the turnarounds; don't start phrases too loud |
| **Singing flat** | Sing a bit brighter, more "forward," with more breath energy. Check your starting note. Make sure your guitar is **in tune** |
| **Strumming too loud to hear yourself** | Strum lighter, or use your thumb. Hand over one ear |
| **Strumming stops when you sing** | Go back to the [Layering Method](04-Singing-While-Playing.md#2-the-layering-method-the-core-technique): hum first, then speak the words, then sing over whole notes |
| **Dm sounds bad** | Strum only strings 4–1. Check that finger 3 isn't muting string 1 |

## 10. Listening homework (do it all month)

Listen to the original at least **once a day**. Each time, focus on **one thing**:
1. Count along: "1 2 3 4." Feel where beat 1 is.
2. Tap your foot and notice the chord changes (can you hear the move to Dm?).
3. Listen only to the bass instrument.
4. Listen only to the voice. Where does he breathe?
5. Count the bars in the "I know" section.
6. Notice where each line starts relative to the chord (on the beat, or before it?).
7. Listen to the strings (the orchestral arrangement). How do they add to the mood?
8. Sing along quietly.
9. Listen to 2–3 **cover versions** (many artists have covered it). How do they change the feel? Notice that none of them sounds like Withers, and that's fine.
