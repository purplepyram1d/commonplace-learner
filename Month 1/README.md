# Month 1 — Acoustic Guitar & Singing (From Zero to "Ain't No Sunshine")

> **Goal for the month:** By the end of Day 28 you will play and sing *"Ain't No Sunshine"* (Bill Withers, 1971) all the way through, from beginning to end, without stopping, and you'll have a recording of it.

This folder is a complete 4-week course for someone who has **never played guitar and never trained their voice**. It covers:

- The guitar on its own (holding it, tuning it, your first chords, strumming, changing chords)
- Your voice on its own (breathing, posture, warming up, matching pitch, keeping your voice healthy)
- Both at the same time (the hardest part, and the one most beginner courses skip)
- The one song everything builds toward

---

## Why this song is a good first song

| Reason | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Only **4 chords** (Am, Em, G, Dm), and 3 of them are among the easiest on the guitar | You can play it after about 2 weeks |
| **Slow tempo** (around 76–80 BPM) | You have time to move your fingers between chords |
| **Short**: about 2 minutes | You can run it many times in one practice session |
| **Narrow vocal range** | You won't be reaching for high notes |
| **Repetitive structure** | Once you know one verse you know most of the song |
| The famous **"I know, I know…" section** sits on one chord | It's good practice for singing over a steady strum |
| A minor key and a bluesy feel | It sounds good even when played simply |

## The daily time commitment

| Week | 🌙 Post-shift day | 🟢 Off day |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20–30 min | 30 min |
| 2 | 25–30 min | 35 min |
| 3 | 30 min | 45 min |
| 4 | 30 min | 45–60 min |

**Short daily sessions beat long weekly ones.** Twenty minutes every day is far better than two hours on Sunday. Your fingertips need daily, gentle exposure to toughen up. Your brain also consolidates motor skills during sleep, so every night's sleep after practice is part of the practice.

You can split a session in two (e.g. 15 minutes morning, 15 minutes evening). That works just as well, and some people find it works better.

## The four rules of this month

1. **Slow is fast.** Every mistake you practice at speed gets learned too. Play slowly enough to get it right, then speed up.
2. **Pain is a stop sign; discomfort is normal.** Sore fingertips are expected. Sharp pain, pain in the wrist or forearm, or a hoarse or painful voice means stop for the day. (See [Troubleshooting](07-Troubleshooting-and-FAQ.md).)
3. **Record yourself from Week 1.** Use your phone. You can't hear yourself accurately while you're playing and singing. The recording tells you what actually happened.
4. **Don't skip the boring stuff.** Tuning, warm-ups, and one-minute chord changes are what separate people who can play in a month from people who quit in a month.

## Milestone checklist for the whole month

Copy this into your log and tick items off as you go.

### Week 1
- [ ] I can tune my guitar with a tuner in under 2 minutes
- [ ] I can play **Em** with every string ringing clearly
- [ ] I can play **Am** with every string (except the low E) ringing clearly
- [ ] I can switch Em ↔ Am at least 15 times in one minute
- [ ] I can do 4 counts in / 8 counts out belly breathing without lifting my shoulders
- [ ] I can hum a note that matches the open A string (tuner or app confirms)

### Week 2
- [ ] I can play **G** (full or easy version) cleanly
- [ ] I can play **Dm** cleanly
- [ ] I can switch between any two of my 4 chords at least 20 times in a minute
- [ ] I can strum 4 steady down-strums per bar with a metronome at 70 BPM without drifting
- [ ] I can count "1 2 3 4" out loud while strumming without stopping
- [ ] I can sing a 5-note major and minor scale in tune (checked against a recording)

### Week 3
- [ ] I can play the full song chord progression from start to end with the chord chart
- [ ] I can sing the full melody with a recording (no guitar)
- [ ] I can hum the melody while strumming the progression
- [ ] I can sing Verse 1 while playing whole-note strums (one strum per chord)

### Week 4
- [ ] I can play and sing the complete song with a simple strum
- [ ] I can play and sing the complete song with the main strum pattern
- [ ] I recorded a full take with no stops
- [ ] I played it for at least one other person (or on a video call, or posted it)

## A note on the lyrics

This course does **not** reproduce the song's full lyrics, because they're copyrighted. Get them from a licensed source: the lyrics display in Spotify or Apple Music, the album liner notes, or a licensed lyrics site such as Musixmatch or LyricFind-powered services. The song guide gives you a line-by-line map of chords, breaths, and timing that you can lay over the lyrics.

Listen to the original recording (*Just As I Am*, Bill Withers, 1971) as often as you can this month: in the car, while cooking, on walks. **Active listening is practice.**
